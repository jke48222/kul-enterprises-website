Chapter Three · The Founder’s Story

Why I Started
Driving.

Every company has a story. Ours began long before the first truck. It began with character — and it began on an island.

Told in seven acts

This is not a résumé. It is the road that led here — the summers, the service, the setbacks, and the years behind the wheel that turned a set of lessons into a company.

Told in the founder’s own words. Nothing exaggerated — the story carries its own weight.

I
Act I · Jamaica · August 8, 1988

An island, and a
one-way summer

“I was born in Jamaica.”

“My mother brought my sister and me to America. She told us we were spending the summer with our father.”

“We never went back.”

The lesson

Sometimes life’s greatest journeys begin without our permission.

Act II · Learning the value of work

While most kids
spent summers playing

“Mine were spent on construction sites. Every school break. Every holiday. Every vacation. I worked alongside my father.”

At the time, he didn’t understand why. He only knew that it was expected.

“Work isn’t something to avoid. It’s something to take pride in.”

The lesson

Character is often built long before opportunity arrives.

III
Act III · The beginning of independence

Every decision,
finally his own

“Leaving home wasn’t just about finding a place to live. It was about finding out who I was.”

“Some were good. Some weren’t. But every one of them belonged to me.”

“Independence isn’t the freedom to avoid mistakes. It’s accepting responsibility for them.”

The lesson

Growth begins the moment excuses end.

USAFUnited States Air Force
Service and commitment

Act IV · Choosing a greater challenge

Forged through
discipline

“I wanted structure. I wanted to be challenged. That’s what led me to the United States Air Force.”

“It demanded consistency. Accountability. Commitment.”

“Discipline isn’t about being controlled. It’s about learning to control yourself.”

The lesson

Character grows when comfort is replaced with commitment.

“Excellence is rarely built in a single moment. It’s built one decision at a time.”

A lesson the Air Force left for good
V
Act V · Life doesn’t follow a straight line

Victories and setbacks,
both teachers

“I thought I knew where life was taking me. Like many people, I had a plan. But plans have a way of changing.”

“Some opportunities opened new doors. Others closed before I expected. There were victories. There were setbacks. Both became teachers.”

“Life rarely asks whether you’re ready. It simply asks — what will you do now?”

The lesson

Resilience isn’t refusing to fall. It’s choosing to keep moving.

VI
Act VI · Earning trust

Leadership,
earned quietly

“I wasn’t chasing titles. I was learning to become dependable.”

“One responsibility became another. One opportunity led to the next. Before long, I found myself leading others.”

“Leadership isn’t about being in front. It’s about being someone others can count on.”

The lesson

Trust is earned long before it’s ever expected.

VII
Act VII · About eleven years of commercial driving

The road became
my teacher

“I thought I was learning how to drive a truck. What I didn’t realize was that the road was teaching me something far greater.”

“Every mile introduced me to a new place. Every state introduced me to different people. Every delivery reminded me that trust travels farther than freight.”

“The country was changing. And so was I.”

The lesson

The road doesn’t just take you to new places. It teaches you to see the world differently.

Every mile taught a lesson.
Every lesson helped build KUL Enterprises.

Chapter Three · The Founder

Every road has a beginning.Why I Started
Driving

This isn't a biography. It's the miles that made a standard — told the way the road taught them: one lesson at a time.

Begin

Mile One · 1988Jamaica → America

“I was born in Jamaica.” “On August 8, 1988, my mother brought my sister and me to America.” “She told us we were spending the summer with our father.” “We never went back.”

A journey that began without warning, without a map, and without permission — the first of many.

Sometimes life's greatest journeys begin without our permission.

Mile Two · The Value of WorkConstruction sites, every school break

“While most kids spent their summers playing...” “Mine were spent on construction sites.” “Every school break. Every holiday. Every vacation.” “I worked alongside my father.”

At the time, he didn't understand why. He only knew it was expected. Looking back, those days taught something that couldn't be seen then: work isn't something to avoid — it's something to take pride in.

Character is often built long before opportunity arrives.

Mile Three · IndependenceLeaving home

“For the first time, I was making my own decisions.” “Some were good. Some weren't.” “But every one of them belonged to me.”

Leaving home wasn't just about finding a place to live. It was about finding out who he was. Independence isn't the freedom to avoid mistakes — it's accepting responsibility for them.

Growth begins the moment excuses end.

Mile Four · A Greater ChallengeUnited States Air Force

“What kind of man did I want to become?” “I wanted structure. I wanted to be challenged.” “That's what led me to the United States Air Force.” “Discipline isn't about being controlled. It's about learning to control yourself.”

The Air Force demanded consistency. Accountability. Commitment. Every day became another opportunity to become a little stronger than the day before. It didn't change who he was — it revealed who he was willing to become.

Character grows when comfort is replaced with commitment.

Mile Five · No Straight LinesPlans change

“I thought I knew where life was taking me.” “But plans have a way of changing.” “There were victories. There were setbacks.” “Both became teachers.”

Some opportunities opened new doors. Others closed before he expected. Life rarely asks whether you're ready — it simply asks: what will you do now?

Resilience isn't refusing to fall. It's choosing to keep moving.

The destination was never visible from here.

He kept driving anyway.

Mile Six · Earning TrustLeadership, earned

“I wasn't chasing titles.” “I was learning to become dependable.” “One responsibility became another. One opportunity led to the next.” “Before long, I found myself leading others.”

Leadership isn't about being in front. It's about being someone others can count on.

Trust is earned long before it's ever expected.

Mile Seven · The Road Became My TeacherRoughly eleven years of commercial driving

“I thought I was learning how to drive a truck.” “What I didn't realize was that the road was teaching me something far greater.” “Every mile introduced me to a new place. Every state, to different people.” “Trust travels farther than freight.”

Roughly eleven years behind the wheel of a commercial truck. Mountains, deserts, cities that never seemed to sleep, and quiet roads where time felt like it had stopped. The country was changing — and so was he.

The road doesn't just take you to new places. It teaches you to see the world differently.

Every mile taught a lesson.
Every lesson helped build KUL Enterprises.

The company came later. The character came first. That order is the whole point — and it's the standard every load rides on today.

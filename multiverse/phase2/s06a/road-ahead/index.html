§02 — The gate

A truck has to earn
its way into this fleet.

Growth is a decision we make slowly on purpose. Before tractor number two — and before every tractor after it — the same four questions get answered in writing.

G-01
Does the standard hold?Pre-trip, post-trip, ELD discipline, documented maintenance — at the current size, with margin to spare. If the checklist is straining at one truck, it breaks at ten.
G-02
Is the driver's seat worth sitting in?A seat gets added only when it can be a good job — equipment kept tight, dispatch that answers, respect as policy. Drivers are the fleet; the tractor is just where they work.
G-03
Does dispatch quality survive the addition?One more truck must never mean one less phone call answered. Communication scales first, capacity second.
G-04
Is there a customer promise behind it?Capacity follows relationships, not the other way around. Every added truck should trace back to work we were trusted with and delivered.

Field note

The number is ambition. The standard is not negotiable.

If the gate slows the curve down, the curve waits. A fleet of fifty run loosely is worth less than one truck run to spec — to us and to you.

§04 — The horizon

Small is a stage,
not a weakness.

Today, the founder drives the truck, plans the route, and answers the phone. Whatever the fleet count says in 2029, that attention is the product — and the gate exists to keep it that way at fifty.

The plan is not to get big. The plan is to stay good while getting bigger.

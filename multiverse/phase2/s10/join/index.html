Chapter Six · Join the Journey

The seats are coming.
The standard is already here.

Here’s the honest version: KUL runs one truck, and the owner is driving it. We’re not hiring yet — we’re building a company that will be worth driving for when we are.

Not hiring today · Preparing for fleet growth · Opportunities announced as tractors are added

How seats open

Growth first. Hiring follows.

Today

One truck. Owner behind the wheel.

Every mile, every inspection, and every delivery is currently handled by the founder. That’s not a limitation we’re hiding — it’s the standard every future driver will inherit.

As the fleet grows

Every tractor added is a seat announced.

We add trucks when the freight earns them — no overreach, no leased-on shortcuts. Each new tractor means a new driver opportunity, announced here and to everyone on the notify list first.

By end of 2029

Fifty tractors. Fifty drivers who chose well.

That’s the goal we’ve set publicly, and we intend to reach it the same way we run freight: honestly, safely, one mile at a time.

The company we’re building

What driving for KUL will mean.

No invented pay tables, no benefits we haven’t built yet. These are the commitments the company is being constructed around — because the founder drives, and he’s building the carrier he always wanted to work for.

Equipment

You’ll drive a truck we’d put our own name on.

Maintained on schedule, inspected every run, grounded when something’s wrong. No driver at KUL will ever be pressured to run questionable equipment.

Hours

Your hours of service are yours.

Routes planned within legal hours from the start. Rest is part of the schedule, never a negotiation. A carrier that games your clock is gambling with your life — we won’t be one.

Respect

Dispatch will know your name, not just your truck number.

This company is being built by a driver. The distance between the person in the cab and the person making decisions will stay short — that’s the point of growing carefully.

Honesty

What we promise is what you’ll get.

Pay, benefits, and home time will be published plainly when hiring opens — real numbers, in writing, before you commit. If we can’t offer it honestly, we won’t offer it.

Worth the wait

The best seat in trucking is one built slowly.

We could hire fast and burn people out like the carriers we left behind. We’d rather take the time to be different.

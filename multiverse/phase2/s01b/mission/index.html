What we haul, and how.

Written for the people who book trucks. If a service fits your load, the quote is one call away. If it doesn't, we'll say so.

Trailer & equipment

  • Power Only

    Good for: preloaded trailers, drop yards, surge capacity

    Your trailer, our tractor. We hook to your loaded or empty trailer and move it where it needs to go. Drop and hook or live load, clean fifth-wheel connections, and no wasted dock time on either end.

  • Dry Van

    Good for: general commodities, palletized freight, retail

    Enclosed 53-foot freight, dock to dock. The workhorse of the industry, run carefully: load secured properly, seals intact, paperwork clean, and the receiver never guessing where the truck is.

  • Reefer

    Good for: food, beverage, anything with a temp range

    Temperature-controlled, watched the whole way. Set point confirmed at pickup, monitored in transit, documented at delivery. The cold chain is treated as part of the load, not an assumption.

Commitment & range

  • Dedicated

    Good for: recurring lanes, steady weekly volume

    A truck that learns your operation. Same equipment, same standard, week after week. Your docks, your hours, and your people stop being new information and start being routine.

  • Regional

    Good for: Southeast lanes, short and mid-length hauls

    Home ground, known mile by mile. Based in Loganville, Georgia, we run the Southeast as our primary territory: Georgia and its neighbors, with lane knowledge you only get from living here.

  • Over-the-Road

    Good for: long-haul, coast-to-coast moves

    Nationwide, with rest planned in. Long hauls are scheduled around legal, honest hours of service, so the arrival window we give you is one we can actually keep.

When the clock is the load

  • Expedited

    Good for: line-down freight, hard appointment times

    Straight through, with updates before you ask. When a load absolutely cannot be late, it gets a direct run, proactive check calls, and a dispatcher who treats your deadline like our own.

Chapter three · The founder's story

Why I started
driving.

Not a biography. A road, told in the miles that mattered, in our founder's own words.

Jamaica

The journey began without my permission.

"I was born in Jamaica."

"On August 8, 1988..."

"My mother brought my sister and me to America."

"She told us we were spending the summer with our father."

"We never went back."

Sometimes life's greatest journeys begin without our permission.

The value of work

Summers on construction sites, beside my father.

America was different. Everything moved faster, and everything felt unfamiliar. While most kids spent their summers playing, his were spent on construction sites. Every school break. Every holiday. Every vacation. Working alongside his father.

"At the time, I didn't understand why."

"I only knew that it was expected."

"Work isn't something to avoid."

"It's something to take pride in."

Character is often built long before opportunity arrives.

Every school break.
Every holiday.
Every vacation.

A photograph from those summers belongs here. Until it's found, the words hold the space.

Seeing the world differently

I wasn't satisfied with being told how things were.

"I wanted to understand why they worked the way they did."

"Questions became part of the journey."

"Curiosity doesn't always give you immediate answers."

"Sometimes, it gives you a lifetime of learning."

Every road begins with a question.

Independence

For the first time, every decision was mine.

Leaving home wasn't just about finding a place to live. It was about finding out who he was. Some decisions were good. Some weren't. But every one of them belonged to him.

"Independence isn't the freedom to avoid mistakes."

"It's accepting responsibility for them."

Growth begins the moment excuses end.

"I wanted structure. I wanted to be challenged. That's what led me to the United States Air Force."

The Air Force demanded consistency. Accountability. Commitment. Every day became another opportunity to become a little stronger than the day before. Not about being controlled: about learning to control yourself.

Forged through discipline

Some lessons can only be learned through discipline.

"Discipline doesn't change who you are."

"It reveals who you're willing to become."

Excellence is rarely built in a single moment. It's built one decision at a time.

Consistency.
Accountability.
Commitment.

A photograph from his service years belongs here. Service and commitment, never hardware.

The road bends

There were victories. There were setbacks. Both became teachers.

Like many people, he had a plan. But plans have a way of changing. Some opportunities opened new doors. Others closed before he expected.

"Life rarely asks whether you're ready."

"It simply asks..."

"What will you do now?"

Resilience isn't refusing to fall. It's choosing to keep moving.

Earning trust

I wasn't chasing titles. I was learning to become dependable.

One responsibility became another. One opportunity led to the next. Before long, he found himself leading others.

"Leadership isn't about being in front."

"It's about being someone others can count on."

Trust is earned long before it's ever expected.

Eleven years on the road

I thought I was learning how to drive a truck.

Roughly eleven years of commercial driving. Every mile introduced him to a new place. Every state introduced him to different people. Every delivery carried the same reminder: trust travels farther than freight.

"What I didn't realize..."

"was that the road was teaching me something far greater."

The road doesn't just take you to new places. It teaches you to see the world differently.

Every mile taught a lesson. Every lesson helped build KUL Enterprises.

The next chapter is what those lessons became: a mission, written down and kept.

# The Journey — coherence review of the 17 scene specs

Produced 30 Jul 2026 by an adversarial pass over all seventeen specs at once.
Each spec was written by an agent that could only see its own scene, so every
finding below is a problem that is invisible from inside a single scene and
only appears when the page is read end to end.

Read this BEFORE building any scene. The build order at the bottom is the plan.

---

## 1. COLLISIONS

**Scene 5 and Scene 6 share the identical Mobbin URL** (`59838174-0f8b-4c85-9985-8541f4e69473`, Faculty Department) and they are adjacent. Both are "quiet editorial type column on one side, photographic panel on the other, the two advancing at different rates." Scene 5 dresses it as a seam that opens and closes; Scene 6 dresses it as a plate that fills. A viewer scrolling 5 into 6 sees the same composition twice in ninety seconds. **Scene 6 changes.** Its actual idea — a drawn line that turns out to have been a horizon — does not need a split at all. Rebuild 6 as a single centred plate on an otherwise empty stage with Mark's six lines set beneath it in one measure, and let the gold rule and the ridgeline be the entire composition. It gets quieter, which the page badly needs at that point, and the split becomes Scene 5's alone.

**Scenes 3 and 11 both cite Freshman**, different sections, but the underlying mechanic is the same state machine: an active index drives which photograph occupies one media well. Scene 3 promotes into a frame from a rail; Scene 11 travels a plate down a name stack. Both are "the archive answers the line you are reading." Given Scene 11's answer is mostly *no photograph*, and given the jobsite asset problem below, **Scene 3 should be the one to change** — see §4.

**Scenes 3 and 9 are the same scene.** Both use jobsite-fixtures / jobsite-install / jobsite-bay, in that order, as three photographs illustrating a three-beat progression, in a media well beside a copy column, where the set accumulates and never empties. Scene 3's `whyUnique` claims "the only scene built as an accumulating ledger, a rail that fills and never empties." Scene 9's claims "the only scene where the frame accumulates rather than replaces, none of them ever leaves." They are describing each other. The same three photographs appear twice, six scenes apart, in three different crops (native 4:3 in 3, native 4:3 in 9, 16:9 in 11). **Cut the photographs out of Scene 9 entirely** and let its eight cards be type-only strata — the stack mechanic survives intact and is stronger without three cards being visually different from the other five.

**Scenes 15, 16 and 17 are three consecutive ruled tables.** Six-row hairline laminate, then a fifty-graduation ruler with a five-tick value rule, then a twenty-six-row index with column heads. All Archivo, all hairlines at low alpha, all on light ground. Worse: Scene 16's Block C prints INTEGRITY / CHARACTER / SAFETY / SERVICE / TRUST as the ruler's units, which is Scene 15's six values restated eight scenes later with two dropped. That is a content repeat inside a form repeat. **Scene 16's Block C must go.** The instrument does not need its units labelled with the values; the stations already carry the method.

**Non-uniform pacing is claimed as unique by three scenes.** Scene 4 ("reveal interval authored as uneven whitespace"), Scene 8 ("scroll-to-content mapping is deliberately non-uniform"), Scene 10 (per-slot deceleration curve), Scene 16 (stepped ramp with holds). Four scenes, one idea. Scene 8's is the weakest expression and the most expensive to build — its curve is doing the work already.

**The light/heavy weight step on a two-clause aphorism** is claimed as a signature by Scenes 12, 14, 15, 16 and 17. Scene 14's spec says "used exactly twice in the scene and nowhere else." It is used in five scenes. Pick one scene to own it.

## 2. RHYTHM

Read in order, the page has no alternation between scenes. It has alternation *inside* scenes, which is the wrong scale — the viewer experiences the seam, not the internal structure.

**Run A, scenes 3–6: four consecutive media-beside-copy splits.** 3 is plate-left/copy-right, 4 is a left-hanging stack, 5 is copy-left/photo-right, 6 is plate-left/copy-right. Only Scene 4 breaks it, and only weakly. Four scenes, roughly 1,200vh, one composition.

**Run B, scenes 8–10: the page's worst stretch.** 400vh curved-path scrub, then 620vh perspective card stack, then 720vh horizontal photo strip. That is 1,740vh — seventeen screens — of continuous, maximum-complexity, pinned scroll-scrubbing with no relief between them. Scene 9 alone spends eight beats at 60vh each. By the time the viewer reaches Scene 10, the longest scene in the film, they are done. **Scene 9 must come down to 400vh or be cut to five cards.** Its own spec offers 520vh as a fallback; that is not enough.

**Run C, scenes 8–14: seven consecutive pinned scenes, none under 340vh.** 400 / 620 / 720 / 540 / 520 / 380 / 340 = 3,520vh. Thirty-five screens with the viewport locked, back to back, no scene in normal flow to breathe. Scene 12's spec states "it must be the longest pin in the film; if any other scene is also over 400svh, shorten that one." Scenes 8, 9, 10, 11 and 16 are all at or over 400vh and Scene 10 is 300vh longer than Scene 12. The specs are issuing each other contradictory instructions because nobody read across.

**Run D, scenes 11–13: three consecutive mid-tone type scenes.** 11 has two photographs in a 480px well, 12 has none, 13 has none. All three pinned, all three at the tightest contrast on the page. After the 8-9-10 marathon this reads as the page running out of material, not as restraint.

**There are almost no short scenes.** Only Scene 1 (260vh), Scene 7 (~190vh) and Scene 15 (~100vh) come in under 300vh. Total page is roughly 6,400vh, sixty-four screens, six to ten minutes of scrolling for a one-truck carrier's story. Cut a thousand vh minimum, and it should come out of 9, 10 and 11.

**Thirteen consecutive scenes end the same way.** Scenes 2 through 14 all close with an Archivo `LESSON` label, a rule, and a two-clause Newsreader aphorism, left-aligned. Thirteen in a row. No individual spec notices because each only looks at itself. This is the single most template-shaped thing in the document and it is precisely the "PowerPoint" charge the client named. Eight of those aphorisms are also the identical "X isn't A, it's B" construction. **Vary the closing device or drop the label from at least half of them.** Some scenes should simply end.

**Eleven scenes contain a deliberate void that their own risk section admits will read as broken** (2, 4, 6, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17 explicitly; 7, 11, 15 by implication). Each argues its emptiness is the one unique silence. Scene 2 claims to be "the only place in the entire seventeen-scene document where the reader travels more than half a screen through nothing" — Scene 13's empty lane is 163vh, Scene 12's frozen pause has literally zero pixel change for 32vh, Scene 16 opens with a dead hold. Silence at eleven occurrences is not a gesture, it is a mannerism.

## 3. FLOW

Nothing carries across the seams except the ground colour, and two scenes make handoff promises the receiving scene breaks.

**Scene 12 promises "the accent returns in scene 13 with first daylight."** Scene 13's own contrast check computes #D6A145 at 1.21:1 on its ground and forbids gold as text, icon, tick or any mark read against the ground. The promise is factually wrong. Gold does not actually return anywhere until Scene 16, and when it does it returns as a *different colour* (#A05C08).

**Scene 16 ends deliberately right-aligned "so the eye exits to the right where scene 17 begins."** Scene 17's entire governing idea is a left manuscript margin with everything anchored at x=72 and x=336. The handoff is stated and then contradicted by the receiving scene.

**The time stamp — one of only two declared invariants — is non-monotonic in six places.** In order: 03:41, 04:12, 04:41, **04:12**, ?, **04:12**, 04:41, 04:41, **03:40**, 03:50→15:10, 06:04, **05:41**, 06:40, 07:58, 10:52, **10:40**, **09:12**. Scene 9 runs earlier than Scene 1. Scene 10 burns to mid-afternoon and Scene 11 resets to dawn. The film ends at 09:12 having already been at 15:10. The clock is the archival furniture's only claim to being a record and it does not survive a single read-through. Fix it as a page-level table before a single scene is built; no scene may choose its own value.

**Scene 5 does not specify its time stamp at all.** It says "the time-of-day stamp." Blocker.

**Hard cuts that are undeliberate:** 10→11 (full-bleed sharp photograph resolving, straight into a mid-grey type index — this one is actually good, write it down and keep it on purpose); 14→15 (a truck coming to rest, straight into a six-row ruled table — nothing carries, and the emotional payoff of 14 is thrown away by the next seam); 7→8 (monumental justified type into a curved path, no bridge).

**The only real handoff in the document** is Scene 2's request that the light arc hold flat through its pause and begin lightening at the top of Scene 3. That is the correct instinct and it is the only instance of it. Every scene should own its exit the way Scene 2 owns its entrance.

## 4. HONESTY

**Scene 3 is a lie and must be rebuilt.** It uses jobsite-fixtures, jobsite-install and jobsite-bay — photographs taken on Mark's phone in **2021–2022**, when he was an adult running a business — to illustrate "While most kids spent their summers playing, mine were spent on construction sites" and "I worked alongside my father." That is the 1990s. The spec's own risk section says "a windscreen shot standing in for a construction site would be a lie about Mark's childhood, which is worse than shipping the scene late" — and then commits exactly that lie with adult photographs. There is no photograph of Mark's childhood summers. **Scene 3 must become a type-only scene**, or a scene whose media well is honestly empty. This is the most serious finding in the review and it also resolves the 3/9 and 3/11 collisions at a stroke. The field note (mark-fieldnote.mp4, hard hat and hi-vis, 2022) goes with it — it is not a document of his childhood either.

**Scene 14's regulatory numbers.** "USDOT 7638788 · MC 66389691" are presented as read "straight off the real door decal." MC numbers do not run to eight digits. Either these were transcribed wrong or invented. Publishing a fabricated operating authority number on a live carrier's site is a legal exposure, not a design note. **Do not build Scene 14 until Mark confirms both numbers from the decal or the FMCSA record.**

**Scene 16's fifty tractors by end of 2029.** Nothing in the brief supplies that number or that date. A one-truck carrier printing a 50x fleet target as a calibrated instrument graduation, with a hard year on it, is the one boast on the page — and it is rendered with the visual authority of a measurement, which makes it worse than stating it in prose. It also sits directly against Mark's rule 3. **Either the client supplies the number and the date on the record, or the endpoint loses its year and the ruler loses its fifty.** The scene works better anyway with an unlabelled right-hand terminus.

**Scene 10 invents eleven time stamps.** Its own risk section correctly forbids inventing mileage labels for the horizon ticks, on the grounds that "it would be the one fabricated fact on a page whose entire argument is that nothing on it is invented" — and then invents eleven clock values in the same component. The stepping clock is a good idea; it cannot be presented in the archival register that everything else on the page uses to signal "this is a record."

**Ten of the twenty-six photographs appear in no scene.** s02b-caribbean-water, gal-alpine-lake, gal-river-rocks, gal-waterfall-figure, s07-pines-road, hero-sage-plain, s09b-elevated-traffic, s10-endless-road, s14-confident-highway, hero-open-road-blue. Scene 17's index lists all twenty-six with a **SCENE** column. Ten of those rows have no scene to put in it. Either the index drops to the plates that actually appear, or ten photographs need homes. Related: **dash-daylight is never used anywhere**, while Scene 10 hand-crops eleven stills to fake a continuous windscreen. That is the wrong way round.

**The plate register is broken in every scene that uses it.** Against Scene 17's index (the only place the register is actually defined), s04-dawn-road-mist is PL. 04 in Scene 4 and PL. 12 in the index; hero-two-lane-centred is PL. 05 and PL. 16; s06-wide-horizon is PL. 06 and PL. 15; s08b-rockcut-bend is PL. 09 and PL. 19; s09-interstate-traffic is PL. 26 and PL. 20; s16-valley-from-height is PL. 24 and PL. 25. jobsite-fixtures is **PL. 07 in Scene 3 and PL. 21 in Scene 9** — the same photograph, two plate numbers. PL. 22 is claimed by both Scene 9 and Scene 14. Scene 10 claims PL. 30 through PL. 40 and Scene 11 claims PL. 27, all outside a 26-plate register. And Scene 17 puts the jobsite photographs and the portraits in an unnumbered addendum, meaning Scenes 3, 9, 11 and 14 have assigned plate numbers to things the index says are not plates. **The register is one of the two invariants and it does not survive contact with itself.** Build it as a single data file first; no scene hardcodes a number.

**Five conventions for "no photograph exists."** Scene 6 uses an in-frame caption reading NO PHOTOGRAPH EXISTS OF THIS DECISION; Scene 7 uses NO PLATE; Scene 11 uses a drawn empty frame reading NO PHOTOGRAPH EXISTS plus a caption reading NO PLATE plus a footnote; Scene 13 uses PL. NONE; Scene 15 silently omits the slot. Pick one. It is furniture.

**The brief says three scenes genuinely have no photograph.** The specs have produced five asset-free scenes (7, 12, 13, 15, 17) plus Scene 6 empty for a third of its run, and Scene 3 will make six once it is fixed honestly. Two of those are inventing an absence rather than reporting one. Scenes 13 and 15 both work as type-only, but they are adjacent to 12 and 17 which are also type-only, and that is the run problem in §2.

**Em dashes** appear in spec prose in Scenes 6 (five times in the typography block), 11 (twice), and 14 (twice). None appear in shipped copy strings, which is what matters, but the specs are the handoff document and this text goes into code comments. Strip them.

## 5. ACCESSIBILITY AND CONTRAST

**Scene 8 is the only scene shipping sub-AA resting text and it is Mark's own words.** Off-anchor statements floor at alpha 0.34 → 2.86:1. The spec is honest about it and offers three mitigations, but every comparable scene solved the same problem by raising the floor: Scene 1 floors at 0.55, Scene 7 at 0.55, Scene 9 at 0.55 *specifically to clear AA*, Scene 10 at 0.50 after testing 0.42 and rejecting it. Scene 8 is the outlier for no reason. **Raise it to 0.55** (≈5.26:1 on #26282a) and lose nothing.

**Scene 12 is worse than Scene 8 and nobody flagged it as the page's floor.** Ghost state at 0.14 → **1.24:1**. Outgrown-question residual at 0.30 → **1.63:1**. And the ground itself only gives 4.99:1 at full ink, so there is no headroom at all: any anti-aliasing loss puts full-strength text under AA. Then b2, the outgrown question, is set at **Newsreader weight 200 at 49px** on that 4.99:1 ground. Weight 200 hairline serif at a 4.99:1 ratio is functionally a light-grey suggestion. The escape hatch requires the reader to *click a control* to get readable text, and `prefers-contrast: more` has thin support. **Scene 12 needs a darker ink than #12141A or the light arc needs to hold a darker ground under it.** As specced it is the least accessible scene on the page and it is the emotional turn.

**Scene 11 got this right and should be the house rule.** It computes 5.18:1, refuses opacity dimming entirely, and carries active state on weight, optical size, translation and a tick instead. Its weight-400 floor at 88px on mid-grey is still a real risk and its own spec says so — that check must happen on an actual sRGB panel, not a retina laptop. But the principle is correct and the page currently has **no policy**: Scenes 1, 3, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 12 all dim with opacity, Scene 11 bans it on principle. Decide once.

**The accent has no cross-page design.** #D6A145 fails on the grounds of Scenes 11 (2.29:1), 12 (1.59:1), 13 (1.21:1), 14 (1.11:1) and 15 (1.44:1). Five consecutive scenes independently rediscover the same arithmetic and each invents its own workaround: 11 spends none, 12 spends none, 13 hides it inside a black bar, 14 hides it inside a caption bar, 15 ships it at 1.44:1 with three paragraphs of justification. Scene 16 then reveals that the codebase already has `--color-gold` at **#A05C08** for light grounds. **That is a page-level token decision and it should apply from Scene 11 onward.** Scene 15 in particular should use it rather than shipping a 1.44:1 mark and arguing about it.

**Scene 3's keyboard interaction silently destroys the scene.** Clicking a rail tile "sets a session lock that suspends scroll-driven promotion for the rest of the visit." The rail uses roving tabindex with arrow-key navigation — so a keyboard user pressing Right, which is the natural first thing to do on focus, permanently kills the mechanic with no way back. **Arrow navigation must move focus without committing the lock; only Enter/Space commits.** (Moot if Scene 3 is rebuilt per §4, but the pattern will get copied.)

**Scene 5 puts `tabindex="0"` on a figure whose only response is a filter change.** That is a tab stop with no operable purpose — it costs a keyboard user a stop and offers nothing. Remove it or give it something to do.

**Hover-only interactions are otherwise handled well.** Scene 4, Scene 7 and Scene 16 all correctly gate on `pointer: fine`, convey no information by hover alone, and lose nothing on touch. Scene 9 gives full focus parity plus a touch path. Scene 8's focus-scrolls-to-anchor is the best pattern in the document and should be copied wherever a scene has off-screen content.

**Reduced motion is the strongest part of this set.** Scene 3's contact sheet, Scene 9's flow list, Scene 10's contact sheet, Scene 11's six permanent plate slots and Scene 12's 96px translation of the pause are all genuinely complete, not degraded. Two notes: Scene 10 is right that branching on `useReducedMotion()` ships the 720vh track to everyone and swaps on hydration — that must be a CSS-only switch and it applies to every pinned scene, not just 10. And Scene 12's warning that `components/k/PinnedStatement.tsx` currently renders at 0.16 from the server and relies on hydration is the exact failure the motion rules exist to prevent; that component should be fixed before it gets reused.

## BUILD ORDER

Build the spine before any scene. **Step 0 is not a scene:** a single harness containing the light-arc ground ramp across seventeen empty sections, the furniture component, the time-stamp table as one data file, and the plate register as one data file. Every finding in §3 and §4 above exists because seventeen specs each invented their own copy of these. Prove the ramp and the clock scroll continuously through sixty-four screens of nothing before a single word is set.

**Act I — The Origin (scenes 1, 2).** Build first. Scene 1 proves the darkest end of the arc, the entrance-visible-by-default pattern and the furniture. Scene 2 carries the most important image on the site and the hardest single decision in the document (the 56vh pause). If the client rejects the pause, that verdict changes Scenes 4, 12, 13 and 16 before they are built. Reviewable as an act; ends on "We never went back."

**Then jump forward and build Scene 14 out of order.** It is the payoff, it proves the lightest end of the arc, and it de-risks the two things most likely to sink the project: whether a red-flooded backstage phone snapshot can carry the emotional climax, and whether the USDOT/MC numbers are real. Build it early enough that a bad answer to either is survivable.

**Then build Scene 10 out of order.** 720vh, eleven photographs, a hand-measured horizon-registration table, a full-bleed blurred backdrop and ~20MB of decoded texture on mobile. It is the longest and most expensive scene, it owns the night-to-day crossover, and if its performance budget does not hold on a mid-range Android the entire middle of the page needs replanning. Do not discover that in month two.

**Act II — Formation (scenes 3, 4, 5, 6, 7).** Build after Scene 3 has been re-specced honestly and Scene 6 has been rebuilt off the split. This act contains Run A and it is where the four-splits-in-a-row problem gets fixed or does not. Ends on the discipline monolith, which is the strongest pure-type moment in the film and a genuine act curtain.

**Act III — The Road (scenes 8, 9).** Build last of the heavy scenes, with 10 already standing so the 8-9-10 fatigue can be judged in situ rather than argued about. Scene 9 should be built at its reduced length from the start; do not build 620vh and then cut it.

**Act IV — The Company (scenes 11, 12, 13).** Build after the accent token decision from §5 is made, since all three depend on it. Scene 12 needs its ground or its ink resolved before it is built at all.

**Act V — The Institution (scenes 15, 16, 17).** Build last, together, as one act — because they are three tables and the only way to see whether that is a resolution or an ending that dribbles out is to look at all three at once. Scene 17's index cannot be built until the plate register from Step 0 is final and the ten homeless photographs have been resolved.

**The natural act breaks are: after 2, after 7, after 10, after 14.** Four curtains: the origin, the formation, the crossover, the reveal. Scene 10 is the hinge of the whole film — it is where night becomes day — and it should be reviewed on its own, not inside an act.

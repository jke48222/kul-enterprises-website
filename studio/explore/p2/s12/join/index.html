The standard, both directions

A KUL driver is held to something. So is KUL.

We can’t show you a pay package we haven’t set or a schedule we haven’t built. What we can show you is exactly how this truck is run today — and that’s the same standard the fiftieth one will be run by.

What we’ll ask of you

Drive it like it matters.

  • Safety before scheduleIf a load can’t be run legally and safely, it doesn’t get run. That call is yours to make out there, and we back it.
  • Honest hours, honest logsNo creative math. You will never be pushed by this company to invent time you don’t have.
  • A real pre-trip and post-tripEvery day, on paper and in practice. The walk-around is the job, not paperwork about the job.
  • Communication before we’re guessingA call when something changes beats an explanation at the dock. Dispatch would always rather know early.
  • Respect at the gateAt the shipper and the receiver you are the company. How you carry yourself is our reputation.
  • Care for the equipmentTreat it like the only truck the company has — because right now, that’s exactly what it is.
What you can expect from us

Get treated like it matters.

  • Straight answersIncluding “we don’t know yet.” You shouldn’t have to decode a sales pitch from your own carrier.
  • A dispatcher who picks upThe same 24/7 line our customers call. No ticket queue, no phone tree, no waiting until Monday.
  • Never asked to run wrongNot illegal, not unsafe, not dishonest. If a customer demands that, we lose the customer — not the driver.
  • Maintenance as a safety itemRepairs get handled because someone has to sit in that seat tomorrow, not because an inspection is coming.
  • The plan, not the rumorWhen the fleet grows or a lane changes, you hear it from us first and plainly.
  • Respect, both directionsYou’ll be spoken to like a professional who chose this work. That’s the whole deal.

What isn’t on this page: pay rates, benefits, and home-time policy. Those get published when a real opening is published — written down, specific, and honest. Anything sooner would just be a number invented to hold your attention.

Where the seats come from

One truck today. Fifty tractors by the end of 2029.

Every tractor added is a seat, and every seat needs somebody who drives the way we say we drive. That’s the whole reason this page exists before the jobs do — so the right people find us early, while the company is still small enough to know everyone in it.

We won’t put a date on your seat that we can’t keep. We’ll put the opening here the moment it’s real.

When a seat opens

Three things we’re committing to now.

You should be able to judge a carrier by how it hires, not only by how it advertises. So here’s how ours will work — written down before it happens.

01

It gets posted here first.

This page and the notify list are where a KUL opening appears. It won’t sit on a job board for a week before reaching the people who actually followed the company.

02

The posting will be specific.

Lanes, schedule, equipment, and pay written into the posting itself. You shouldn’t need an interview to find out the basics of the job.

03

You get a straight answer.

If you apply and it isn’t a fit, we’ll tell you that, and tell you why. Silence is the cheapest thing a company can do, and we’re not interested in it.

Don’t want to wait for a posting? Introduce yourself.

Tell us what you drive, where you run, and what you’re looking for. When a seat opens, we’d rather already know your name than start from a stack of applications.

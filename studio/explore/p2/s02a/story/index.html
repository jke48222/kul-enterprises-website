(Chapter 03) — The Founder’s File Told in his own words · Eight folios

Why I Started Driving

Every company has a story. Ours began long before the first truck — it began with character. What follows is the founder’s road, told the way it happened: without shortcuts, and without exaggeration.

The road begins Jamaica → America

Every road has a beginning·This is mine·
Every road has a beginning·This is mine·

(I) Jamaica   (II) Work   (III) Questions   (IV) Independence (V) The Air Force   (VI) Setbacks   (VII) Leadership   (VIII) The Road

(I) — Jamaica Where the story starts

(Folio I) — We Never Went Back

“I was born in Jamaica.”

“My mother brought my sister and me to America. She told us we were spending the summer with our father.”

“We never went back.”

08·08·1988 The day the journey began — arrival in America, August 8, 1988
The lesson

Sometimes life’s greatest journeys begin without our permission.

(II) — Learning the Value of Work Construction summers, alongside his father

(Folio II) — Summers on Site

“America was different. Everything moved faster. Everything felt unfamiliar.”

“While most kids spent their summers playing, mine were spent on construction sites. Every school break. Every holiday. Every vacation. I worked alongside my father.”

“At the time, I didn’t understand why. I only knew that it was expected.”

“Work isn’t something to avoid. It’s something to take pride in.”

The lesson

Character is often built long before opportunity arrives.

(III) — Seeing the World Differently Curiosity as a compass

(Folio III) — Questions Became the Journey

“As I grew older, I began to notice something. I wasn’t satisfied with simply being told how things were. I wanted to understand why they worked the way they did.”

“Curiosity doesn’t always give you immediate answers. Sometimes, it gives you a lifetime of learning.”

The lesson

Every road begins with a question.

(IV) — The Beginning of Independence Every decision his own

(Folio IV) — Where Excuses End

“Leaving home wasn’t just about finding a place to live. It was about finding out who I was.”

“For the first time, I was making my own decisions. Some were good. Some weren’t. But every one of them belonged to me.”

“Independence isn’t the freedom to avoid mistakes. It’s accepting responsibility for them.”

The lesson

Growth begins the moment excuses end.

(V) — The United States Air Force Service and commitment, not hardware

(Folio V) — Forged Through Discipline

“Independence gave me freedom. But freedom also brought a question: what kind of man did I want to become?”

“I wanted structure. I wanted to be challenged. That’s what led me to the United States Air Force.”

“The Air Force challenged me in ways I had never experienced before. Every day became another opportunity to become a little stronger than the day before.”

  • (A)Consistency
  • (B)Accountability
  • (C)Commitment

“Discipline doesn’t change who you are. It reveals who you’re willing to become.”

The lesson

Excellence is rarely built in a single moment. It’s built one decision at a time.

(VI) — Life Doesn’t Follow a Straight Line Honesty over perfection

(Folio VI) — Both Became Teachers

“I thought I knew where life was taking me. Like many people, I had a plan. But plans have a way of changing.”

“Some opportunities opened new doors. Others closed before I expected. There were victories. There were setbacks. Both became teachers.”

“Life rarely asks whether you’re ready. It simply asks — what will you do now?”

The lesson

Resilience isn’t refusing to fall. It’s choosing to keep moving.

(VII) — Earning Trust Leading by being dependable

(Folio VII) — Someone Others Can Count On

“I wasn’t chasing titles. I was learning to become dependable.”

“One responsibility became another. One opportunity led to the next. Before long, I found myself leading others.”

“Leadership isn’t about being in front. It’s about being someone others can count on.”

The lesson

Trust is earned long before it’s ever expected.

(VIII) — The Road Became My Teacher Commercial driving, coast to coast

Folio VIII — roughly eleven years of commercial driving

Eleven years
on the road

Roughly eleven years behind the wheel of a commercial truck. “I thought I was learning how to drive a truck. What I didn’t realize was that the road was teaching me something far greater — that trust travels farther than freight.”

(End) — Why It Matters The file closes; the company begins

Every mile taught a lesson. Every lesson helped build KUL Enterprises.

The character in these folios is the standard on every load we’re trusted with today. If that’s the kind of carrier you want on your freight, the door is open.

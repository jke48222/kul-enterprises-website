The Founder’s Story

Why I started driving.

Before there was a company, there was a road. This is the story of how it was built — told honestly, in seven chapters, because the trust we ask for deserves nothing less.

Jamaica, 1988 U.S. Air Force Eleven years on the road
Chapter One

Jamaica.

“I was born in Jamaica.”

“On August 8, 1988, my mother brought my sister and me to America.”

“She told us we were spending the summer with our father.”

“We never went back.”

What it taught

Sometimes life’s greatest journeys begin without our permission.

Chapter Two

Learning the value of work.

“America was different. Everything moved faster. Everything felt unfamiliar.”

“While most kids spent their summers playing, mine were spent on construction sites.”

“Every school break. Every holiday. Every vacation.”

“I worked alongside my father.”

“At the time, I didn’t understand why. I only knew that it was expected.”

“Work isn’t something to avoid. It’s something to take pride in.”

What it taught

Character is often built long before opportunity arrives.

Chapter Three

Seeing the world differently.

“As I grew older, I began to notice something.”

“I wasn’t satisfied with simply being told how things were.”

“I wanted to understand why they worked the way they did.”

“Curiosity doesn’t always give you immediate answers. Sometimes, it gives you a lifetime of learning.”

What it taught

Every road begins with a question.

Chapter Four

The beginning of independence.

“Every life reaches a moment when someone else stops making the decisions. This was mine.”

“Leaving home wasn’t just about finding a place to live. It was about finding out who I was.”

“Some decisions were good. Some weren’t. But every one of them belonged to me.”

“Independence isn’t the freedom to avoid mistakes. It’s accepting responsibility for them.”

What it taught

Growth begins the moment excuses end.

Chapter Five

Choosing a greater challenge.

“Independence gave me freedom. But freedom also brought a question.”

“What kind of man did I want to become?”

“I wanted structure. I wanted purpose. I wanted to be challenged.”

“That’s what led me to the United States Air Force.”

“It demanded consistency. Accountability. Commitment.”

“Discipline doesn’t change who you are. It reveals who you’re willing to become.”

What it taught

Excellence is rarely built in a single moment. It’s built one decision at a time.

Chapter Six

Life doesn’t follow a straight line.

“I thought I knew where life was taking me. Like many people, I had a plan.”

“But plans have a way of changing.”

“Some opportunities opened new doors. Others closed before I expected.”

“There were victories. There were setbacks. Both became teachers.”

“Life rarely asks whether you’re ready. It simply asks — what will you do now?”

What it taught

Resilience isn’t refusing to fall. It’s choosing to keep moving.

Chapter Seven

Earning trust.

“Opportunity has a way of finding people who are prepared for it.”

“I wasn’t chasing titles. I was learning to become dependable.”

“One responsibility became another. One opportunity led to the next.”

“Before long, I found myself leading others.”

“Leadership isn’t about being in front. It’s about being someone others can count on.”

What it taught

Trust is earned long before it’s ever expected.

Then came the road

The road became the teacher.

Roughly eleven years behind the wheel of a commercial truck. Every mile introduced a new place. Every state introduced different people. Every delivery was a reminder that trust travels farther than freight.

~11 years of commercial driving

Every mile taught a lesson.
Every lesson helped build KUL Enterprises.

The company that carries this name was not built in an office. It was built on job sites, in uniform, through setbacks, and across eleven years of American highway — one decision at a time.

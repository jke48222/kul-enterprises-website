The distance,

Two numbers are written down and neither is flattering. Everything between them has to be earned the way the first truck was.

[ Fleet meter ] — One square, one tractor

01 filled · 49 unbuilt

Most carriers draw this as a curve, because a curve draws the part that has not happened yet. Squares cannot flatter anybody. Forty-nine of them are empty today, and every one of them has to be filled by freight that already exists — not by a slide.

002 / The route

Five stages.
We are on the first.

Each stage names what has to be true before it earns a tractor, and one thing we will not do to get there. Four of the five have not started.

[ Stage 01 of 05 ]

In progress — we are here

Prove the standard

One tractor, run flawlessly: inspected before every pull, hours logged legal, the phone answered by a person. The first tractor is the audition for every tractor after it — and it is the only one a shipper can judge us on right now.

What has to be true

  • [ A ]Every pull begins with a walked pre-trip, on the good days and the late ones.
  • [ B ]Hours are logged legal. No schedule is worth a shortcut, and none has been.
  • [ C ]Dispatch is answered by a person, at any hour, including the awkward ones.

What we will not do Add a second truck to look bigger than we are.

[ Stage 02 of 05 ]

Not started

Earn repeat lanes

Growth is funded by kept promises — shippers who call back because the last load arrived the way we said it would. No lane is added faster than it can be run right, and a second lane never comes at the cost of the first.

What has to be true

  • [ A ]Shippers call back without being chased. A repeat call is the only score we keep.
  • [ B ]Every lane is still quoted by the person who dispatches it.
  • [ C ]The truck we already have is busy before the fleet gets any bigger.

What we will not do Buy volume with a rate we cannot run safely.

[ Stage 03 of 05 ]

Not started

Add trucks deliberately

A tractor joins the fleet when the work behind it is already dependable — never to look bigger, only to serve better. Each addition carries the same pre-trip, the same insurance and the same standard as the first.

What has to be true

  • [ A ]The freight exists before the truck does — not the other way round.
  • [ B ]Maintenance is funded before the payment is, so nothing runs tired.
  • [ C ]The published count matches the count in the yard, on the day it changes.

What we will not do Finance a tractor against freight we only hope to win.

[ Stage 04 of 05 ]

Not started

Grow the people first

Drivers join the way customers do: on trust. Every seat we add belongs to someone who treats your freight the way the founder treated his first load — which means the seat waits for the right person rather than the first one.

What has to be true

  • [ A ]Pay and home time are stated plainly before the interview ends.
  • [ B ]The standard is taught by someone who runs it, not handed over as a document.
  • [ C ]No seat is opened that we cannot keep busy and keep safe.

What we will not do Fill a truck with a driver we would not put our own name behind.

[ Stage 05 of 05 ]

Not started

Arrive at fifty, unchanged

By the end of 2029 the plan reads fifty tractors — with the same pre-trip, the same straight answers and the same person-first standard we run today. Arriving is not the achievement. Arriving unchanged is.

What has to be true

  • [ A ]The fiftieth truck meets the first truck’s standard, without exception.
  • [ B ]A human still answers the phone at three in the morning.
  • [ C ]The count we publish is still exactly the count we run.

What we will not do Let scale become the excuse for any of the above.

No stage above invents a date or a count — only the two written numbers

003 / The ledger

Two columns,
kept in balance

A plan is only worth reading if it also says what it refuses to trade away. The right-hand column is the one that makes the left-hand column believable.

What grows

Everything here is expected to change between 2026 and the end of 2029.
  • [ A ]The fleet From one tractor toward fifty by the end of 2029.
  • [ B ]The lanes More of the country served directly, without a broker maze in between.
  • [ C ]The team Drivers and dispatch added seat by seat, on trust.
  • [ D ]The relationships Every kept promise compounds into the next call.

What never changes

Nothing here is on the table at any size, in any market, on any lane.
  • [ A ]The pre-trip, every trip Safety is not a department. It is how the truck leaves the yard.
  • [ B ]Hours run legal, always No schedule is worth a shortcut, and none ever will be.
  • [ C ]A human answers 24 / 7 dispatch, straight numbers, no runaround and no queue.
  • [ D ]The honest count We will always tell you exactly how big we are.

The principles that will guide us there

  • Integrity
  • Character
  • Safety
  • Service
  • Trust

We don’t know every opportunity ahead — we do know the principles that meet it

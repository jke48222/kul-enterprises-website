1988

Arrival

Where the story starts

“I was born in Jamaica. My mother brought my sister and me to America. She told us we were spending the summer with our father.”

“We never went back.”

A one-way trip that nobody called a one-way trip. The date is the one fixed point in the whole file — everything after it is a decision.

Stamped — 08 · 08 · 1988 · Arrival in America

Sometimes the greatest journeys begin without your permission.

The summers

On site

Learning the value of work

“America was different. Everything moved faster. Everything felt unfamiliar.”

“While most kids spent their summers playing, mine were spent on construction sites. Every school break. Every holiday. Every vacation. I worked alongside my father. At the time I didn’t understand why. I only knew that it was expected.”

“Work isn’t something to avoid. It’s something to take pride in.”

[ F—00 ] — Portrait, not yet made · The frame stays reserved

There is no photograph of the founder on this page yet.

Every other frame in this chapter is his own — taken on his own time, unretouched, and captioned honestly. A portrait held to that standard has not been taken. Rather than borrow a stock face or fill the space with an illustration of a man who isn’t him, the frame is left as an open record: sized, catalogued, and waiting.

It is the same rule the rest of the site runs on. One tractor is one tractor. An unmade photograph is an unmade photograph. Nothing on this page is a stand-in for something we do not have.

Signed

Mark

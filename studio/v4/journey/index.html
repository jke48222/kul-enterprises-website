Jamaica to a construction site in America. Nobody asked the boy whether he was ready; the work taught him anyway.

Jamaica

I was born in Jamaica.

On August 8, 1988…

My mother brought my sister and me to America.

She told us we were spending the summer with our father.

Long pause

We never went back.

[ The lesson ]

Sometimes life’s greatest journeys begin without our permission.

Learning the Value of Work

America was different.

Everything moved faster.

Everything felt unfamiliar.

While most kids spent their summers playing…

Mine were spent on construction sites.

Every school break…

Every holiday…

Every vacation…

I worked alongside my father.

At the time…

I didn’t understand why.

I only knew that it was expected.

Hold

Looking back…

Those days taught me something I couldn’t see at the time.

Work isn’t something to avoid.

It’s something to take pride in.

The Beginning of Independence

Every life reaches a moment…

When someone else stops making the decisions.

This was mine.

Leaving home wasn’t just about finding a place to live.

I was making my own decisions.

Some were good.

Some weren’t.

But every one of them belonged to me.

Independence isn’t the freedom to avoid mistakes. It’s accepting responsibility for them.

Four frames

The Road Became My Teacher

I thought I was learning how to drive a truck.

What I didn’t realize…

was that the road was teaching me something far greater.

Every mile introduced me to a new place.

Every state introduced me to different people.

Every delivery reminded me…

That trust travels farther than freight.

  1. I saw mountains… [ J—01 ] — Bluff country over open water Plate one of four: a flat-topped mesa rising above bright turquoise water.
  2. deserts… [ J—02 ] — Sandstone country Plate two of four: tall sandstone rock formations standing over an expanse of empty sand.
  3. cities that never seemed to sleep… and quiet roads where time felt like it had stopped. [ J—03 ] — Back country, still water Plate three of four: a turquoise creek winding through dense green forest.
  4. The country was changing. And so was I. [ J—04 ] — Headland surf Plate four of four: a wave breaking against a sandstone headland on an open beach.

The farther I traveled…

the more I realized…

Every person has a story.

[ The lesson ]

The road doesn’t just take you to new places. It teaches you to see the world differently.

A Vision Begins to Form

The longer I traveled…

The more I began to notice something.

Freight moved every day.

But what truly kept the industry moving…

wasn’t trucks.

It was people.

  • [ 01 ]Drivers
  • [ 02 ]Dispatchers
  • [ 03 ]Mechanics
  • [ 04 ]Customers
  • [ 05 ]Brokers
  • [ 06 ]Families waiting at home

Every load represented someone’s trust.

Every mile represented someone’s promise.

That’s when a vision began to take shape.

Long pause

What if a company could be built…

Where integrity wasn’t just a word…

but the standard?

A business begins with an idea. A lasting business begins with values.

Nothing was registered yet. There was no authority, no truck and no name on a door — only a driver noticing, load after load, that the industry runs on people keeping their word.

More Than a Business

There came a moment…

when I stopped asking…

What kind of business do I want to own?

Long pause

I began asking a different question.

What kind of company deserves to exist?

  • [ 01 ]A company built on honesty… when honesty costs something.
  • [ 02 ]A company built on integrity… even when no one is watching.
  • [ 03 ]A company where safety isn’t measured by regulations alone… but by the value placed on every person who trusts us.

I realized…

I wasn’t trying to build a trucking company.

Long pause

I was trying to build something people could believe in.

Businesses are created every day. Trust is built one decision at a time.

Every company begins with paperwork.

But that’s not where KUL began.

KUL began with a promise.

  • [ 01 ]A promise… to do business with integrity.
  • [ 02 ]To treat people with respect.
  • [ 03 ]To never compromise safety for convenience.

That promise became the foundation.

The trucks would come later.

A company’s reputation isn’t created by advertising. It’s created by the decisions no one else sees.
